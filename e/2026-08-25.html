Losers: US exporters on the list and border-straddling supply chains; winners: EU and Mexican substitutes, Canadian domestic producers. Market fact: the S&P 500 was slightly higher Tuesday midday with yields falling — equities keep pricing the trade war as contingent and reversible, exactly as they did when the 338 duties took effect.

Why it matters: Canada has moved from threats to a dated, priced list — September 8 is now the de facto deadline for a deal, and January 1 (autos/steel at 50%) is the next rung on the ladder.
