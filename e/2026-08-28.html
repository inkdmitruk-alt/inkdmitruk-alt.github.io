Why it matters: The front end and the long end are now telling the same story for the first time in weeks — both point to higher-for-longer. Watch whether the 30-year pushes back above 5.3%: a hawkish Fed plus fiscal supply is the combination that turns an equity valuation problem into a credit problem.

Lambda raises $1bn in debt for chips — the AI cycle is still credit-fundedTechCrunch

Neocloud Lambda secured $1bn of debt to buy more GPUs, the latest instance of compute being bought on credit rather than cash flow. Structurally this is the same trade as Broadcom's roughly $80bn SPV with Apollo and Blackstone: hardware owned by a financing vehicle, leased to AI workloads, funded by private credit and insurance balance sheets. While revenue grows at Nvidia's reported pace the structure is sound; it is sensitive to two things simultaneously — a demand slowdown and a higher cost of capital, which is precisely the combination Friday's Fed repricing made more likely.

🌐 Geopolitics & the Economy 1 item

Russian refining at a 20-year low after 21 strikes on refineries in August; Rostov ports haltedForbes Ukraine / Bloomberg

Bloomberg counts 21 Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries during August, pushing Russian crude processing to its lowest level in two decades. On Friday Rostov region declared a state of emergency after drone attacks halted port operations, disrupting navigation in the Azov-Black Sea basin and grain exports with it. Ukraine also struck a refinery in Yaroslavl region roughly 1,000km from the front.

The economic transmission runs two ways: less refining capacity means Russia exports more crude and fewer products, while damaged ports constrain the export of both — and the same corridor carries a meaningful share of world grain.

Why it matters: Watch diesel cracks and Black Sea freight insurance rather than headline crude — that is where this campaign shows up first in global prices.

🚢 Trade, Tariffs & Policy 1 item

50% duties on Canadian dairy, alcohol and autos live since Aug 22 — first use of Section 338 since 1930Federal Register

The additional 50% duties on Canadian dairy, alcohol and automobiles took effect on August 22 after the three-day pause under Proclamation 11056 expired with no deal. They are imposed under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930, a provision that permits additional charges up to 50% ad valorem and that has essentially never been used in the modern era — which is itself the story, since it sidesteps the procedural constraints of the more familiar Section 232 and 301 routes. Ottawa's counter-tariffs on roughly $20bn of US goods take effect September 8.

Why it matters: The legal novelty is the risk: a Section 338 challenge would be a first-impression case, and an adverse ruling would unwind the tariffs and trigger refunds — the same pattern that just flattered retail earnings through IEEPA rebates.

Sources: Federal Register: proclamation on Canadian tariffs
