🧭 The throughline

Markets are in a regime led by long-dated bonds. The 10-year touched 5.041%, a 19-year high, as part of a global sell-off that pushed the G7 average to a 2008 high, so bond yields, not earnings, are now setting prices across assets. Why it matters: above 5%, the 10-year reprices mortgages, corporate refinancing and debt-funded AI capex all at once, and with oil and yields moving in lockstep the bond market is effectively trading the Strait of Hormuz. What to watch: whether the 10-year closes above 5% today, demand at this week's 20-year reopening, and the Saudi pipeline restart (Rystad sees only a 5-7 day inventory cushion).

Pentagon puts the Iran war at over $42bn and its watchdog confirms munitions shortfalls; an unfunded restocking bill lands on a strained Treasury marketFiscal

A congressional aide told The Hill on Tuesday that the Pentagon told defense committees on Aug. 13 the Iran war has cost more than $42 billion. That is $4.5 billion above the $37.5 billion Defense Secretary Hegseth gave in July. Outside estimates are far higher: Harvard's Linda Bilmes puts it near $100 billion, and NBC reported $80-100 billion in July citing officials. The Defense Department inspector general's first report on the war (released Monday) counts $33.4 billion from Feb. 28 to June 30, excluding base repairs.

That is $22.3 billion in expended munitions, $3.7 billion in equipment losses (four F-15Es and an A-10 destroyed, an F-35A damaged, 30+ drones lost) and $7.4 billion in other costs. The report confirms "strategic inventory shortfalls" and industrial-base bottlenecks, contradicting Trump's claims of virtually unlimited supply. Restocking is unfunded. The White House asked for more than $87 billion in June (about $67 billion for munitions and operations), and the House passed a $95 billion package in July that is blocked in the Senate by Democrats.

Trump also wants a record $1.5 trillion FY2027 defense budget, but the window for moving $350 billion through reconciliation has largely closed before the midterms. For bonds, that means more deficit-financed supply on top of national debt above $40 trillion, landing in a 10-30-year sector where CNBC described a buyers' strike since late June. Bessent testified Tuesday at the House Financial Services Committee.

He defended Treasury's buybacks, doubled to at least $4 billion per operation in the 10-20 and 20-30-year sectors from Sept. 9 through Nov. 4, saying yields would otherwise be higher and citing two of the strongest auctions in 20 years. Ranking Democrat Maxine Waters countered that the Treasury sell-off continues. Bessent said the 10-year partly reflects the need to cut the deficit and that he is working on plans; CNBC's account includes no figures.

Missile-defense and munitions makers gain from multi-year restocking: a July CSIS estimate found some Patriot and THAAD stocks depleted by more than half. Taxpayers (about $1,200 per household, per the left-leaning CAP) and long-bond holders lose.

Why it matters: war spending is the fiscal line growing in real time. A $67-95bn supplemental would be financed with new debt just as Treasury is buying back long bonds to steady the market, so investors are being asked to absorb more duration while demand is weak. What to watch: any Senate movement on the supplemental, the next Pentagon cost update, and the Nov. 4 quarterly refunding (buyback sizes and coupon issuance).

Tanker hit near Hormuz while Saudi Arabia's bypass pipeline stays shut: Brent back above $107 intradayEnergy · Hormuz

On 15 Sept, Oman's Maritime Security Centre said the Panama-flagged tanker ELGAIA was being towed to an Omani port. The ship was about 103 nautical miles north-east of Musandam, and a fire broke out in its engine room after an earlier attack. An Omani navy vessel took off 23 crew; two are still missing. The two sides blame each other. Iran's Revolutionary Guards say the ship hit a naval mine in a restricted area.

US Central Command says an Iranian missile disabled it last month and an Iranian drone hit it again at the weekend. India's foreign ministry counts 14 Indian sailors aboard, 13 rescued and one missing; nobody has yet squared that with Oman's figures. The attack comes while Saudi Arabia's 1,200 km East–West pipeline to the Red Sea port of Yanbu is still shut after drone strikes on 10–11 Sept, which Al Jazeera says were launched from Iraq's Maysan province.

The pipeline can carry 7m barrels a day (b/d) and had recently been moving about 4–5m. Vantor satellite images released on 13 Sept show a pumping station blackened by fire, with crude spilled into the desert. Experts told BBC Verify on 15 Sept the shutdown could cut supply by as much as 3.6m b/d. Repair estimates are 3–5 weeks (officials cited by Common Dreams) to 5–6 weeks (Reuters sources cited by Al Jazeera), but Yanbu holds only 5–7 days of stored crude.
