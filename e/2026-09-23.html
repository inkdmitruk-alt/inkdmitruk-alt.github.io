🧭 The throughline

Wednesday was a rates day that dressed up as a growth story: the hottest S&P Global flash PMI in five years, a badly tailed $70 billion 5-year auction and a Fed governor calling more hikes 'likely' pushed the 10-year Treasury to 5.13%, its highest since 2007, and the move spilled straight into Japanese bonds, mortgages, gold and bitcoin. Equity leadership is dangerously thin (breadth at dot-com-peak levels, Russell 2000 −1.8%), so the index is now hostage to a handful of AI names just as the cost of capital resets higher; the near-term tells are Micron's Sept. 30 earnings, the Trump–Xi summit outcome and whether oil holds above $100.

📊 Macro & Rates 5 items

10-year Treasury yield spikes to 5.13%, the highest since July 2007NBC News / Yahoo Finance

The benchmark 10-year yield rose as high as roughly 5.13–5.14% on Wednesday, its highest level since July 2007, according to NBC News and Yahoo Finance. The move was about 15 basis points in a single session, which NBC described as the largest one-day jump since April 9, 2025. The 5-year yield climbed about 19 bp to around 5.03%, the highest since 2006, while the 30-year traded near 5.37–5.42% and the 2-year near 4.90–4.95%.

The selloff was bear-flattening at the front and steepening at the long end at the same time — a sign that both Fed expectations and term premium were being repriced. Stocks responded mechanically: the S&P 500 fell 0.75% to 7,706.03, the Nasdaq 1.13% to 26,936.04 and the Dow 0.68% (−352 points) to 51,511.59, with rate-sensitive utilities and REITs among the worst sectors. NBC reported the average 30-year fixed mortgage rate jumped to 7.26%, its highest since January 2025.

Why it matters: a 5%+ risk-free rate raises the discount rate on every long-duration asset — growth stocks, real estate, crypto — and tightens financial conditions for housing and small caps without the Fed having to move again.

Sources: NBC News — Treasury yields surge to near 20-year high as oil jumps back above $103 · Yahoo Finance — Dow, S&P 500, Nasdaq tumble as 10-year yield surges to 2007 high · Eurasia Business News — Treasury selloff sends 10-year above 5.1%

Flash PMIs blow past forecasts: composite 58.4, fastest expansion in over five yearsS&P Global via Yahoo Finance / NBC

S&P Global's September flash composite PMI jumped to 58.4 from 56.0 in August, well above the 55.3 consensus cited by Yahoo Finance. The services PMI rose to 58.7 (expected 55.8, prior 56.5) and manufacturing to 57.0 (expected 53.7, prior 53.9). S&P Global said business activity growth accelerated for a fourth straight month to the fastest pace in more than five years, and input costs rose at the steepest rate in four years, according to NBC News.

That combination — accelerating demand plus accelerating cost pressure — is exactly what a central bank fighting above-target inflation does not want to see. The print landed on top of an August payrolls gain of 162,000 that had already beaten estimates. Together they undercut the idea that last week's hike would be the last.

Why it matters: this is the data point that turned a drift higher in yields into a rout — it tells the Fed the economy can absorb more tightening and that cost pass-through is still building.

Sources: Yahoo Finance — Stock market today, Sept. 23 · NBC News — Treasury yields surge to near 20-year high

Weak $70B 5-year auction tails 3.1 bp — among the worst on recordTFTC / Invezz / Reuters via Tradingview

The Treasury's $70 billion 5-year note sale stopped at a high yield of 5.033%, tailing the when-issued level by about 3.1 bp, which TFTC described as the second-largest tail on record for the tenor. The bid-to-cover ratio dropped to 2.212, and primary dealers were left holding about 15.8% of the issue — roughly $11 billion the market did not want. Indirect bidders, a proxy for foreign and institutional demand, took about 54% versus a six-auction average near 65%.

Reuters, cited in coverage of the Japanese bond move, called the auction 'poorly received' and said it compounded the PMI-driven selloff. The weak result pushed yields on most maturities to near two-decade highs in afternoon trading. Further coupon supply this week will be watched as a test of whether buyers return at 5%+.

Why it matters: soft auction demand signals that the marginal buyer of US debt now needs a higher yield, adding a supply/term-premium leg to the rise in rates on top of Fed repricing.

Sources: TFTC — 5-Year Treasury auction yields 5.033%, 2nd biggest tail ever · Invezz — 10-year US Treasury yields hit 2007 highs: here's why

Fed Governor Barr: further hikes 'likely' needed; October hike odds ~70%Reuters via Yahoo Finance / Investing.com

In a speech in Chicago, Fed Governor Michael Barr said 'further policy adjustments are likely to be needed to ensure inflation comes down to target in a timely fashion,' Reuters and Yahoo Finance reported. He said inflation is above 2% and 'not clearly trending toward target,' while risks to the labor market 'have receded.' Barr listed tariffs, the Middle East war, Russia's war on Ukraine and the AI investment boom as successive price shocks.

The FOMC unanimously raised its policy rate to 3.75–4.00% last week — its first hike since July 2023 — and 16 of 18 officials signaled at least one more hike this year. After Barr spoke, the implied probability of a 25 bp October hike rose from roughly 55% to about 70% on CME FedWatch, per Eurasia Business News and Yahoo Finance. Barr is a governor with a vote, so his tilt carries weight.

Why it matters: when a centrist voter signals more hikes into a 5% 10-year, the market loses its 'Fed put' — equity multiples and crypto leverage are being priced against a rising, not falling, policy path.

Sources: Yahoo Finance (Reuters) — Fed's Barr says further rate hikes will likely be needed · The Hill — Fed's Barr says future rate hikes 'likely' needed · FXStreet — Barr: further rate hikes needed

Global contagion: Japan's 10-year JGB hits 3.055%, highest since 1996Reuters via Tradingview / Nikkei Asia

Japan's benchmark 10-year government bond yield rose 8 bp to 3.055% early Thursday Tokyo time, its highest since August 1996, Reuters reported, directly following the overnight Treasury selloff. A weaker yen added to Japanese inflation concerns; the dollar traded around ¥158.26 and the dollar index held above 100, per Investrade. Reuters described the US move as the sharpest daily rise since last year's 'Liberation Day' rout.

Japan is the largest foreign holder of Treasuries with nearly $1.2 trillion, so the two markets reinforce each other: higher JGB yields make it more attractive for Japanese institutions to keep money at home. Pressure has been spreading across Canada, the eurozone, the UK and South Korea as well.

Why it matters: if Japanese investors repatriate as domestic yields reach 3%, a key source of Treasury demand weakens exactly when auctions are already tailing — a feedback loop for global term premium.

Administration officials are weighing a rollback of the plan to expand beef imports as Republican midterm anxiety spreads, Politico reported. Why it matters: a reversal would keep US beef prices — a visible food-inflation item — elevated and signals that trade policy is being set by electoral math, which makes tariff relief less dependable for importers and restaurant chains.

BitMEX, the perpetual-swap pioneer, shuts trading after 11 yearsCoinDesk / Cointelegraph

BitMEX ended trading, deposits and new positions after an 11-year run, leaving only withdrawals open, CoinDesk and Cointelegraph reported. The closure follows a strategic review by parent HDR Global Trading announced in July. API withdrawals will be disabled on Sept. 28, after which users must withdraw via the web platform. BitMEX helped create the perpetual swap, now the dominant crypto derivatives instrument. Its exit continues consolidation of offshore derivatives venues into a few large players and regulated onshore exchanges.

Why it matters: liquidity migrates to fewer venues, increasing concentration risk in crypto derivatives, while regulated US platforms (CME, Coinbase, Kalshi) gain share.
