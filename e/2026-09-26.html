🧭 The throughline

The market is running two contradictory stories at once: equities finished the week higher on AI earnings optimism while the bond market priced in a higher-for-longer world, with the 10-year Treasury yield touching 5.23%, its highest since 2007. The bridge between the two is oil: Brent near $105-106 keeps inflation sticky, which keeps an October Fed hike (roughly 67-70% priced) on the table, which in turn pushes long yields up. Saturday's rejection by President Trump of Iran's seven-day Hormuz plan removes the most obvious relief valve for that chain, and markets have not yet traded it. The regime is therefore 'fragile risk-on': stocks are tolerating expensive money as long as AI profits grow faster than rates, and next week's Micron report and macro data will test whether that holds.

📊 Macro & Rates 4 items

10-year Treasury yield touches 5.23%, highest since 2007; 30-year near 5.48%CNBC / The Hill

The benchmark 10-year yield reached 5.23% on Friday, a 19-year high, while the 30-year bond traded around 5.48%, close to levels last seen in 2004. Three forces are pushing at once: sticky inflation fed by oil above $100, heavy Treasury issuance to fund deficits, and an AI capital-spending boom that competes for savings. Futures now price roughly 67-70% odds of another Fed hike in October.

The long end is where the pressure is concentrated, which signals investors demanding more term premium rather than only reacting to near-term Fed policy. Higher long yields flow directly into 30-year mortgage rates, corporate borrowing costs and equity valuation multiples. Some investors are starting to see these levels as an entry point for bonds.

Why it matters: a sustained break above 5.25% on the 10-year would raise the discount rate on every long-duration asset — from growth stocks to real estate — and test the equity rally.

Sources: CNBC · The Hill

Fed hiked to 3.75-4.00% in September and projects 3.7% PCE inflation by year-endFederal Reserve

At its Sept. 16 meeting the FOMC voted unanimously to raise the fed funds range by a quarter point to 3.75-4.00%, citing persistent inflation and 'geopolitical developments'. The median projection for headline PCE inflation at end-2026 was raised by a full point to 3.7%, with core at 3.4%, and a return to 2% not expected until 2029. Real GDP growth for 2026 was nudged up to 2.3%, meaning the Fed is fighting inflation in an economy that is not slowing much.

J.P. Morgan expects one more hike in December, while market pricing leans toward October. A hiking Fed alongside strong growth is the classic setup for rising long-term yields.

Why it matters: the Fed is now explicitly tightening into an oil shock, so energy prices have become a direct input into the rate path.

Sources: Federal Reserve SEP · FRED Blog

Treasury's expanded long-bond buybacks (up to $6B per operation) fail to cap yieldsBloomberg / CNBC

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent more than doubled the size of long-end 'liquidity support' buybacks from Sept. 9 through Nov. 4, targeting the 10-20 and 20-30 year sectors that had seen a buyers' strike since late June. This week Treasury again planned to buy up to $6 billion of longer-dated debt. When first announced on Aug. 19, the move knocked the 30-year down 9 basis points, but yields have since climbed to new highs.

Strategists argue buybacks can smooth liquidity but cannot fix the underlying mix of deficits and inflation. Bloomberg's Eric Balchunas links the program to this week's surge of cash into bitcoin ETFs.

Why it matters: if the market keeps overpowering buybacks, pressure grows on Treasury to shift issuance toward bills — a policy lever worth watching in the November refunding.

Sources: Bloomberg · CNBC
