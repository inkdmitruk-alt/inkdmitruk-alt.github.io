Index futures were up roughly 0.3%-0.6% early Friday ahead of payrolls, with the VIX near 16.

Why it matters: Long yields above 5% raise mortgage, corporate and federal borrowing costs at the same time; if payrolls surprise hot, the retreat could reverse quickly and pressure rate-sensitive sectors and small caps.

ISM prices paid surge to 77.9 as jobless claims fall to 197,000, keeping Fed tightening in playQuartz / Saxo / FXStreet

The ISM manufacturing index eased to 54.5 in September from 54.6, below the 55.0 consensus, but marked a ninth straight month of expansion. NY Fed President Williams said there is no need for urgency, and Vice Chair Jefferson urged patience while noting inflation remains elevated.

Why it matters: Input-cost pass-through from energy and tariffs threatens to reverse the PCE relief, so the Fed's choice is less whether to hike again than when, which keeps the front end of the curve and the dollar supported.

Eurozone inflation jumps to 3.8%, a three-year high, on an 18.8% energy surgeEuronews / CNBC / Eurostat

Eurostat's flash estimate showed eurozone inflation at 3.8% y/y in September, above the 3.6% forecast and the highest in three years. Prices rose 0.6% m/m from August. Why it matters: The ECB faces a stagflationary bind: hiking into an energy shock squeezes indebted governments like France and Italy, while holding risks de-anchoring expectations, which is why Europe's bond and FX markets are now the stress point.

On October 1 the US Treasury moved against the A7 Network, a Russian payment system led by sanctioned fraudster Ilan Shor and state-owned Promsvyazbank. Why it matters: Choking A7 raises transaction costs for both Russian and Iranian trade, widening discounts on their crude and pushing payments into slower, costlier channels, while banks and crypto platforms face heavier compliance screening.

ECB's Schnabel outlines three models for putting central bank money onchainThe Block / ECB

ECB Executive Board member Isabel Schnabel set out three options at the Bank of England's Future of Money conference. In the first, the central bank would issue reserves directly on a programmable platform. In the second, the ECB keeps its real-time gross settlement system and links it to DLT platforms through an interoperability layer without tokenizing reserves. In the third, reserves would be tokenized to fully back private settlement tokens.

Schnabel said central bank money would stay at the core of settlement within the two-tier system. The ECB's Pontes and Appia projects are already testing tokenized settlement infrastructure.

Why it matters: Offering central bank settlement onchain would give tokenized securities in Europe a risk-free settlement asset, reducing reliance on dollar stablecoins and shaping where tokenized markets develop.
