🧭 The throughline

Friday exposed a split-screen economy: hiring has nearly stalled at 29,000 jobs a month, yet long-term borrowing costs keep climbing toward levels last seen in 2007. Equity investors chose the friendly half of that picture, pricing out an October Fed hike and pushing the Nasdaq to a record on AI leaders. The bond market chose the other half, keeping the 10-year near 5.28% because oil above $100 and heavy federal borrowing threaten inflation regardless of payrolls. Washington is now using energy diplomacy - forcing a G7 reserve release, floating a Lukoil deal with Moscow, touting Korean money for Alaska LNG - to manage prices ahead of the midterms. The risk is that a cooling labor market meets sticky inflation, the classic stagflation squeeze in which the Fed has no comfortable move.

📊 Macro & Rates 4 items

September payrolls rise only 29,000; prior two months revised down by 60,000Hoodline / Deseret News / Yahoo Finance

The Labor Department reported 29,000 new jobs in September, far below the 80,000-90,000 economists expected. July was revised to a loss of 10,000 jobs and August cut from 162,000 to 133,000, a combined downgrade of 60,000. Unemployment rose to 4.2% from 4.1%, keeping it within a 4.1%-4.3% band for the seventh month. Gains were narrow: health care added 17,000, construction 11,000 and manufacturing 9,000.

Average hourly earnings rose just 0.1% on the month and 3.0% on the year to $37.81, the slowest annual pace since May 2021 and below August inflation of 3.4%.

Why it matters: Real wages are now shrinking, which threatens the consumer spending that has carried growth, and it weakens the case for further Fed tightening.

Sources: Hoodline - September jobs report · Deseret News - Jobs growth falters

Markets price out an October Fed hike but still expect one by DecemberCNBC / FXStreet

The Fed under Chair Kevin Warsh raised its policy range to 3.75%-4.00% in September, its first hike in three years, defying President Trump's demands for cuts. After Friday's report, CME FedWatch put the chance of another quarter-point hike on Oct. 28 at about 17%, down from roughly 36% a week earlier; prediction markets Kalshi and Polymarket showed a similar 17% hike probability.

The longer path did not change much: FedWatch still showed odds above 75% for a December hike, and FXStreet cited futures pricing a December move as near-certain. The Fed's preferred inflation gauge is projected at 3.7% for 2026, nearly double the 2% target. In other words, a weak labor market has paused the tightening cycle rather than ended it.

Why it matters: The next CPI print becomes the swing factor; a fuel-driven inflation surprise would revive hike bets and pressure both stocks and housing.

Sources: CNBC - Little chance of October hike · FXStreet - The October Fed hike just died

10-year yield rises to about 5.28% despite weak data; 30-year near 5.6%Yahoo Finance / Trading Economics / Quartz

Treasury yields fell immediately after the jobs report but reversed, with the 10-year closing around 5.27%-5.28%, up 3 to 5 basis points on the day. Earlier in the week the benchmark reached 5.34%, its highest level since 2007. The 30-year bond ended near 5.59%-5.63%. FXStreet calculated that since June most of the rise in 2-year yields came on days with no jobs, inflation or Fed news, pointing to structural drivers such as energy-driven inflation risk and term premium rather than data surprises.

Commentators have noted that 10-year yields have tracked oil prices closely this year, tying bond markets to Middle East developments.

Why it matters: Mortgage, corporate and federal borrowing costs are set off this curve, so higher long yields tighten financial conditions even if the Fed stands still.

Sources: Trading Economics - US 10-year yield · Quartz - Treasury yields after jobs report · Investigative Economics - Bond yields follow oil

⚡ Energy & Commodities 4 items

G7 agrees to release 100 million barrels of diesel and crude from strategic reservesIrish Times / CBC / France 24

Group of Seven members agreed to release 100 million barrels of diesel and crude, with a front-loaded substantial diesel release within 20 days and the remainder over four months. Reports said European governments were weighing about 50 million barrels of diesel, with a similar volume of crude potentially coming from International Energy Agency members. The move followed pressure from Washington and President Trump's threat to ban U.S. diesel exports.

The G7 also pledged not to restrict energy exports among its members. Wholesale New York harbor diesel fell almost 5% to $4.43 a gallon, and European diesel futures dropped 8% to $1,337.75 a tonne, the lowest since early September.

Why it matters: Diesel drives freight and farm costs, so a sustained drop would ease a key inflation channel heading into winter heating demand.

Sources: Irish Times - G7 to release 100m barrels · CBC - G7 nations agree to release oil · France 24 - G7 diesel release

Brent settled at $102.25, down only 6 cents, and slightly higher on the week after rolling to the December contract. Brent has oscillated roughly between $100 and $107 in recent weeks as the U.S.-Iran conflict disrupts Strait of Hormuz flows. A wide WTI discount tends to favor U.S. refiners and crude exporters at the expense of overseas buyers.

Why it matters: Brent, not WTI, sets most global fuel prices, so the reserve release is unlikely to bring broad relief unless Gulf shipping risks ease.

Sources: EnergyNow - Oil ends volatile week mixed · Trading Economics - Brent

Why it matters: With both major Gulf chokepoints under threat, the risk premium in Brent is likely to persist regardless of strategic reserve releases.

🚢 Trade, Tariffs & Policy 3 items

A handful of G20 members reject U.S. statement on industrial overcapacityCNBC / Japan Times / Malay Mail

At a G20 trade meeting in Milwaukee, the U.S. Trade Representative's office said a draft ministerial statement on curbing excess industrial capacity and non-market policies was backed by all but a handful of members. USTR said it was severely disappointed by the lack of consensus but did not name the objectors; China objected to similar language at a finance meeting a month earlier.

Only Mexico and Argentina joined a separate U.S. statement on removing forced-labor goods from supply chains. Overcapacity is shorthand for state-subsidized production, mainly Chinese, that floods markets with cheap steel, EVs and chemicals. The split limits the multilateral cover for U.S. tariffs.

Why it matters: Without G20 consensus, Washington is more likely to act unilaterally through Section 301 tariffs.

Sources: CNBC - G20 countries reject US stance · Japan Times - US severely disappointed

Section 301 probe of 16 trading partners could bring new tariffsWhite & Case / Hinrich Foundation / BusinessToday

USTR launched Section 301 investigations in March into whether 16 partners show structural excess capacity: China, the EU, Singapore, Switzerland, Norway, Indonesia, Malaysia, Cambodia, Thailand, Korea, Vietnam, Taiwan, Bangladesh, Mexico, Japan and India. Comments closed April 15 and hearings ran May 5-8, and the excess-capacity probe remains open. A parallel forced-labor investigation has produced proposed tariffs of 10% to 12.5%.

The case list includes examples such as Chinese steel and underused German chemical capacity. Because it covers most major suppliers, any action could touch the majority of U.S. imports.

Why it matters: Fresh broad tariffs would add to goods inflation just as the Fed is weighing another hike.

Sources: White & Case - Section 301 investigations of 16 partners · BusinessToday - US faces G20 pushback

The G7 statement also committed members to refrain from restricting energy exports to each other. For U.S. refiners, avoiding an export ban preserves access to higher-priced overseas markets. For Europe, the deal secures supply but at the cost of drawing down emergency stocks.

Why it matters: Energy export restrictions are now an explicit bargaining tool, adding policy risk to fuel trade.

Sources: France 24 - G7 members to release diesel after US threat · CBC - G7 agree to release oil
