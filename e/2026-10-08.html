FOMC minutes (Sept 15-16): unanimous hike to 3.75-4.00%, 'another increase would likely be appropriate by year end', inflation risks skewed to the upsideFederal Reserve

The minutes released Oct 7 show a 12-0 vote to raise the target range to 3.75-4.00%, with interest on reserve balances set at 3.90%. Participants judged progress on inflation insufficient, citing energy prices, AI-related demand and core services, and 'generally assessed inflation risk as skewed to the upside'; several saw the current stance as not restrictive or only mildly restrictive.

The key line: most participants thought 'another increase in the target range for the federal funds rate would likely be appropriate by year end', while stressing an open-minded, data-dependent approach. The labor market was judged stable and near maximum employment (unemployment 4.1% in July-August), with risks broadly balanced, though some flagged low hiring and elevated long-term unemployment; the September jobs report since showed only 29,000 payrolls and 4.2%.

Participants warned the longer energy prices stay elevated, the greater the risk of broader price pressures, explicitly referencing Middle East shipping disruptions. Staff projections put inflation back at 2% only in 2029 with GDP growth above potential through 2028. The Desk paused reserve management purchases and some participants urged contingency planning for Treasury market stress while limiting the Fed's footprint.

Why it matters: the Fed has told the bond market it is not done; with core PCE at 3.0% and oil rising again, a December hike is the base case unless CPI surprises low.

Hormuz: 12 tanker attacks in one week (Sept 28-Oct 5), a wartime record; tanker struck north of Qatar with casualties; Gulf exports had run above pre-war levels for half of SeptemberReuters via Ekonomichna Pravda / BBC

Reuters, citing three maritime-security sources, counted at least 12 attacks on tankers carrying crude, LNG or LPG around the Strait of Hormuz between Sept 28 and Oct 5, the most in a single week since the US-Iran war began on Feb 28; the IMO logged 9 incidents, above the previous peak of 8 in mid-July. On Wednesday, UKMTO reported a tanker 51 nautical miles north of Qatar hit by multiple projectiles with casualties, identified by a maritime intelligence firm as the oil and chemical tanker Acers.

The paradox: shipping data show Gulf oil exports ran above pre-war levels for about half of September as producers pushed cargoes out, accepting higher insurance and crew risk. Iran and Oman agreed coordinates for 'safe' transit routes on Oct 7, which Gulf News notes does not mean an open strait. The IEA on Wednesday agreed to accelerate strategic reserve releases and prioritize diesel, which briefly knocked prices lower before the attacks and hurricane news reversed the move.

Diesel is the pressure point: NY Harbor ULSD futures are near $4.80/gal, up another 4% in a day, and Ryanair's CEO says jet fuel now trades ~50% above Brent versus the usual 10-15%.

Why it matters: each attack raises war-risk premiums and freight costs; if attack frequency stays at this level, the $120 Brent scenarios floated earlier in the war come back into play.
