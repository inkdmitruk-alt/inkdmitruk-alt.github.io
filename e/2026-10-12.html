US equity markets are open today but the Treasury cash market is closed per SIFMA's Columbus Day schedule, so Friday's close is the reference point. Why it matters: at 5%+ on the 10-year, the equity risk premium is thin and long-duration assets (tech, crypto, real estate) are priced off a much higher discount rate. Any upside CPI surprise can push yields back toward the 5.31–5.36% highs when trading resumes Tuesday.

Nobel economics prize goes to Robert Hall and Michael Woodford for the micro foundations of macro stabilizationCNBC / CNN / NBC

The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences awarded the 2026 economics prize to Robert E. Hall (Stanford) and Michael Woodford (Columbia) "for microeconomic foundations of macroeconomic stabilisation". Their work links household and firm decisions to economy-wide cycles. It underpins the New Keynesian models that central banks use to set rates. Woodford's 2003 treatise "Interest and Prices" is the intellectual backbone of inflation targeting and forward guidance.

The committee chair said their research provides the theoretical foundation for models used by central banks and governments worldwide. The prize is worth SEK 12 million, shared equally.

Why it matters: forward guidance, which Woodford formalized, is why markets parse every Warsh sentence. With a split Fed ahead of the Oct. 27–28 meeting, communication rather than action may drive yields.

The Financial Stability Board warned that global stablecoins and national frameworks have not been tested under severe market stress or issuer insolvency. 🚢 Trade, Tariffs & Policy 3 items

OFAC General License 135 opens a US door to Russian diesel through April 7, 2027; legality challengedFXStreet / CNBC / Newsweek

After a call with Vladimir Putin, Trump said Russia would supply more than 300,000 tonnes of diesel immediately, 500,000 tonnes in November and about 1 million tonnes per month after that. Treasury's OFAC issued General License 135. It authorizes the sale and import of Russian diesel, including into the US, until April 7, 2027. Critics, including Republican Sen. Thom Tillis, question whether the license conflicts with a 2022 statute restricting Russian energy imports while the war in Ukraine continues.

A bipartisan group of senators urged cancellation on Sunday, and Republicans are reportedly exploring ways to block it. The license is a rare case of sanctions relief used explicitly as a domestic price-management tool.

Why it matters: if the license survives, more diesel supply could narrow crack spreads and pressure US refiner margins. If Congress or the courts block it, the market loses an expected source of supply just as winter demand picks up.

Microsoft unveils Maia 300 and says it will rely mainly on in-house chips for AI inferenceMicrosoft Azure Blog / CNBC / The Verge

Microsoft published the architecture of Maia 300, built on TSMC's N2P process with eight HBM4 stacks per accelerator (288GB, 18 TB/s). Versus Maia 200 it claims 2.5x memory bandwidth, more than 2.5x performance per watt and up to 7x rack-level token throughput. Racks hold 96 accelerators, and superpods scale to 12,288. Target workloads are agentic, long-context inference for Copilot, GitHub Copilot and Foundry.

CNBC reports Microsoft will rely mainly on Maia for inference, reducing dependence on Nvidia GPUs. No deployment date was given, and more details are promised at Ignite on Nov. 17–20. Microsoft previously told investors Maia 200 was 30–40% cheaper to run than leading Nvidia chips, per The Information.

Why it matters: inference is becoming the bulk of AI compute spending. Hyperscalers bringing it in-house caps Nvidia's pricing power over the long term and shifts value toward TSMC and HBM suppliers, which ties back to the memory-stock debate.
