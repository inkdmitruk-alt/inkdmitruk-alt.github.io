🧭 The throughline

The market is caught between two energy shocks running in opposite directions: Washington is forcing barrels into the market (a Russian diesel license, an announced Russia-Ukraine 'energy ceasefire'), while Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia and tanker hits near Hormuz keep a risk premium on crude. That tug-of-war feeds straight into Wednesday's CPI, where consensus sees headline inflation at 3.6% driven by energy while core stays near 2.5%, and that split will decide whether the Fed follows September's hike with another. With 10-year yields above 5.2% and the dollar near an 18-month high, the regime is narrow: capital crowds into mega-cap tech and away from small caps, crypto and anything rate-sensitive. Today's thin Columbus Day session (cash bonds closed) only previews that test; the real repricing comes Tuesday with yields and bank earnings.

Nobel to Robert Hall and Michael Woodford for the foundations of stabilization policyNBC News / NPR / ABC News

The 2026 economics prize went to Robert E. Hall (Stanford) and Michael Woodford (Columbia) for the microeconomic foundations of macroeconomic stabilization. Hall's work shows how spending depends on expected future income, and Woodford built the New Keynesian framework in which central banks steer the economy through expectations about future interest rates. Committee chair John Hassler said their work gives the stable theoretical base for models now used by central banks and governments worldwide.

The award lands as the Fed is hiking into slowing hiring, which is exactly the trade-off their models are meant to manage. Woodford called the award a tremendous honor and a surprise.

Why it matters: forward guidance, the toolkit Woodford formalized, is what Warsh will lean on this week to shape rate expectations without moving the policy rate.

Sources: NBC News: Americans Hall and Woodford win 2026 Nobel economics prize · NPR: Nobel goes to Hall, Woodford for work on boom-bust cycles
