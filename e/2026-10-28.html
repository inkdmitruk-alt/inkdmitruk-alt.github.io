In September the Fed raised the funds rate 25bp to 3.75-4.00% on a 12-0 vote, the first increase in three years; CME FedWatch now shows a 90.6-92.8% probability of no change today and roughly 75-85% odds of a hike at the December 8-9 meeting, which comes with fresh projections. 🚢 Trade, Tariffs & Policy 3 items

DHS added BGI Genomics, MGI Tech and Shenzhen BGI Technology to the Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act entity list, making their imports presumptively barred from the US; this is a customs ban, distinct from the 2023 Commerce Entity List designation.Reuters via Yahoo / NYT

Reuters reports the Department of Homeland Security named the three BGI Group companies for alleged work with Xinjiang's government tied to forced labor. Under UFLPA, goods from listed entities cannot enter the US unless the importer rebuts the forced-labor presumption, a bar few clear in practice; the move extends the 2023 Bureau of Industry and Security export restrictions on BGI subsidiaries into a near-total trade cutoff with the world's largest genomics company.

Beijing rejects the forced-labor accusations. The action lands days after the US banned imports from Chinese genomics firms over data-security concerns and as the EU caps Chinese hybrid-vehicle imports at 350,000 a year.

Why it matters: biotech joins semiconductors and EVs as a sector where the US uses human-rights and security law to decouple supply chains; US labs and hospitals that rely on MGI sequencers face re-sourcing costs, and Beijing's reply could target US agricultural or pharma exporters.

Sources: Reuters via Yahoo — US bans imports from three Chinese genomics companies · NYT — US restricts trade with Chinese genomics company

Wind turbines: the Section 232 proclamation published in today's Federal Register (signed October 22) imposes no new tariff and instead orders 180 days of negotiations with trading partners, leaving further measures open; existing 232 duties on steel, copper and aluminum components stay.Federal Register / EY / Yahoo News

After a Commerce probe launched in August 2025, Trump found that wind turbine imports threaten national security, citing intermittency, cybersecurity of foreign components, radar interference with air-traffic and weather systems, and import dependence. Rather than a tariff, the proclamation directs the Commerce Secretary and USTR to pursue or continue negotiations and report progress within 180 days, with the option of additional measures depending on the outcome.

Trade advisers note developers face uncertainty over project costs and schedules; the industry's main lobby had not issued a statement in the coverage reviewed. The order repeats Trump's criticism of wind as unreliable.

Why it matters: it is the second 232 case this year resolved by talks rather than duties, a pattern that keeps tariff threats as leverage without the inflationary hit before the midterms; for European turbine makers (Vestas, Siemens Gamesa) the 180-day clock becomes a negotiating deadline.

Sources: Federal Register — Adjusting Imports of Wind Turbines and Their Parts · EY — Administration directs trade talks on wind turbine imports · Yahoo News — Trump eschews tariffs for trade talks on wind turbines

China's car exports surged 64% in September to about 1.05 million vehicles while domestic sales fell 26%; factories ran at 70.8% of capacity against an estimated 63 million-unit build-out, and the EU is capping Chinese hybrid imports at 350,000 a year.AP via US News / Rest of World / CNBC

The domestic price war has become an export war: with retail sales down 21.7% in January-September, Chinese automakers are shipping overseas to keep plants running, and plug-in hybrids now account for nearly 29% of their European sales versus 15.2% for pure EVs as they route around the EU's 2024 anti-subsidy duties (17.0% BYD, 18.8% Geely, 35.3% SAIC). A price-commitment framework agreed in January remains unimplemented; the EU's reported understanding with Beijing includes readiness to deploy trade-defense tools if talks fail.

CNBC's piece on 'why China is desperate to ship cars' frames the same mechanism: overcapacity plus deflation at home forces exports, which in turn invite tariffs in Europe, Latin America and Southeast Asia.

Why it matters: Chinese auto deflation is being exported into markets where Stellantis, VW and Renault are cutting costs to survive; it is also a live test of whether the EU's hybrid cap or new duties come first, and a headwind for Korean and Japanese makers already squeezed by US 232 auto tariffs.

At a Corpus Christi rally on October 27 Trump joked the promised $5,000 'Trump Dividend' would buy 'a hamburger and maybe a small dessert' at Mar-a-Lago, which is 'not a good place if you're price-conscious', six days before midterms dominated by affordability.Newsweek / Quartz / Benzinga / CNBC

The dividend, announced in September, promises $5,000 per adult citizen if Republicans keep both chambers; the estimated cost is about $1.2 trillion and Trump has pointed to tariff revenue, which runs far below that for a single round. The remark drew immediate criticism as tone-deaf: an October AP-NORC poll found 84% of voters rank cost of living among their top issues, after Trump earlier called affordability concerns a Democratic 'hoax' and the Iran war then pushed gasoline and diesel higher.

The comment did not move broad markets, but it sharpens the fiscal question hanging over Treasuries: a $1.2 trillion transfer on top of a $70 billion war supplemental with the 10-year at 5%.

Why it matters: bond investors are pricing the midterm outcome partly as a referendum on the dividend; a Republican sweep raises the odds of an unfunded $1.2 trillion stimulus into 3.7% inflation, the kind of policy mix that keeps the Fed hiking.

Two October 22 actions hit the Federal Register today: a Defense Production Act Section 303 determination declaring oil production, refining, pipelines and terminals essential to national defense (with $150 million in DPA funds for refineries), and Executive Order 14438 on housing costs that orders a pilot to cancel mortgage insurance early and lower USDA/VA loan rates.Federal Register / Energy.gov / White House / RISMedia

The DPA memo directs the Secretary of War to make purchases and financial commitments for petroleum exploration, production, gathering and transmission pipelines, storage and marine terminals; it sets no project list or dollar ceiling, but the Energy Department has carved up to $150 million from a $1 billion DPA Title III pot appropriated last year to restart an idled refinery and expand capacity, promising 'millions of gallons per day' without a published figure.

Environmental groups call the related April memoranda unlawful; the stated aim is pump prices, with diesel reported at $6.28 a gallon. EO 14438 'Decreasing Housing Costs for the American People' tells the FHFA to pilot automatic cancellation of mortgage insurance once borrowers reach market-value equity thresholds, to make the title-insurance waiver pilot permanent to cut refinancing costs, creates a Task Force on Manufactured Housing Innovation co-chaired by HUD and FHFA, and directs USDA and VA to lower rates on their single-family loans.

Industry reaction is mixed: welcome for the insurance and refinancing pieces, skepticism that any of it moves purchase affordability when the 30-year mortgage is near 7.4%.

Why it matters: both orders are pre-election affordability signals that work around Congress; the DPA move is a small but real fiscal subsidy to refining margins, and the housing EO shifts cost to mortgage insurers and the GSEs rather than lowering the rate that actually prices homes out of reach.
