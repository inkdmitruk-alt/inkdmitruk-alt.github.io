📊 Macro & Rates 5 items

October payrolls +165,000 vs ~65,000 expected; unemployment falls to 4.1%Reuters via Yahoo Finance / CBS News

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported that nonfarm payrolls rose 165,000 in October, far above the Reuters consensus of about 65,000. September was revised up to +60,000 from +29,000, and August–September combined were revised up by 84,000, lifting the three-month average to roughly 137,000 per month. The unemployment rate dipped to 4.1% from 4.2%, and labor-force participation ticked up.

Average hourly earnings rose about 0.4% on the month and 3.1% year over year — still below the inflation rate, meaning real wages are falling. Professional and business services added 70,000 jobs, but involuntary part-time work jumped by 339,000, a sign that some of the headline strength reflects lower-quality hiring. The report undercuts the 'growth scare' narrative that had built during the AI selloff and shifts the focus back to inflation.

Why it matters: a strong labor market plus falling real wages is the mix that keeps the Fed hawkish — strong data now raises rate risk for equities instead of soothing them.

Sources: Yahoo Finance (Reuters): Strong October jobs report sends yields higher · CBS News

Treasury yields climb: 2-year ~4.52%, 10-year ~4.87%, 30-year ~5.27%; dollar firmsReuters via Yahoo Finance

Right after the jobs data, the 2-year Treasury yield rose about 6 basis points to 4.52%, the 10-year rose about 3 bp to 4.87%, and the 30-year added about 2 bp to 5.27%, per Reuters. Other intraday readings put the 10-year between 4.855% and 4.878%. The bigger move at the short end flattens the curve, which is the classic signature of markets pricing a near-term policy hike rather than higher long-run growth.

The U.S. dollar index rose about 0.7% to around 102.2, reinforcing tighter financial conditions. With the 30-year above 5.25%, mortgage rates and corporate borrowing costs stay elevated just as AI-related capex is being questioned. Higher real yields also weigh on long-duration growth stocks, limiting the scope of the tech rebound.

Why it matters: a 4.9% 10-year and a 5%+ long bond raise the discount rate on every richly valued AI stock and tighten credit for the housing and capex cycle.

Sources: Yahoo Finance (Reuters) · NewsBytes: 10-year yield hits 4.855% after jobs report

Why it matters: the Fed is signaling it will not bail out the AI selloff — a 'Fed put' is absent while inflation runs above wage growth.

FinCEN cuts MEXC off from US financial system over $16 billion in illicit flowsThe Block / Decrypt / Cointelegraph

Treasury's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network on Friday issued an order barring US financial institutions from processing transfers involving Seychelles-incorporated exchange MEXC Global. Treasury alleges MEXC handled at least $16 billion in illicit transactions between May 2021 and May 2026, including about $1.5 billion tied to Russian sanctions evasion. It says MEXC kept dealing with sanctioned Russian exchanges Garantex and Grinex as recently as June 2026 and cited 'severe deficiencies' in anti-money-laundering controls.

The action is part of 'Operation Economic Outcast,' launched in August to cut off firms helping Iran evade sanctions. MEXC held roughly $4.5–5 billion in assets, per DefiLlama and Treasury figures. Expect liquidity to migrate to compliant venues and pressure on other offshore exchanges serving sanctioned jurisdictions.

Why it matters: Washington is using financial sanctions to choke Iran and Russia's crypto channels — a direct economic front of the Iran war and Russia sanctions.

Sources: The Block · Decrypt · Cointelegraph

Treasury and Fed withdraw plan to cut crypto travel-rule threshold to $250The Block / crypto.news

FinCEN and the Federal Reserve withdrew their 2020 proposal to lower the travel-rule reporting threshold for crypto and other transfers from $3,000 to $250. The agencies cited the proposal's age, changes in the industry and a need for fresh public input, leaving the $3,000 threshold in place. The withdrawal is reported to take effect Nov. 9, and a new rule could follow after further consultation.

The move eases compliance costs for exchanges and wallet providers, which would have had to collect counterparty data on far more small transfers. It came on the same day FinCEN cracked down on MEXC, illustrating a two-track approach: lighter rules for compliant domestic firms, harsher enforcement on offshore actors.

Why it matters: lower compliance friction supports US-based exchanges and payment use cases while enforcement shifts toward sanctions evasion.

Sources: The Block · crypto.news

Why it matters: retirement-plan access is a potential long-term structural buyer for crypto, offsetting the current ETF outflows — but adoption will be gradual and politically contested.
