Hormuz still effectively closed to commercial traffic: war-risk cover at 3–10% of hull value vs 0.25% pre-war, Gulf-to-Asia crude freight ~$37/bblUNCTAD / IEA / Khaleej Times

Trackers dated Nov 9 describe the Strait as closed to commercial shipping. War-risk premiums are quoted at 3–10% of hull value (vs 0.25% before the war), i.e. $3–10 million per voyage for a $100 million tanker, and underwriters are reluctant to write spot cover. UNCTAD's Oct 29 report found reduced tanker availability, longer routes and ship-to-ship transfers have lifted freight costs; the IEA put Middle East Gulf-to-Asia crude freight at ~$37/bbl by mid-October, roughly five times pre-war.

Insurers say they need months of stability before normal cover returns, so a diplomatic reopening would not quickly cut shipping costs. Separately, Russia is rationing fuel at home: Rosneft capped sales at 30 litres of 95-octane, 50 of 92 and 100 of diesel per customer in Krasnoyarsk, Karelia, Buryatia, Zabaikalsky and Novosibirsk after Ukrainian strikes on four of its refineries since Oct 30.

Why it matters: even a deal leaves a lagging cost shock in freight and insurance that feeds Asian import prices (see China PPI) and keeps a floor under global goods inflation into 2027.
