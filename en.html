📊 Macro & Rates 5 items

Treasuries rally in the first session after Columbus Day: 10-year ~5.19% (−5 bp), 30-year ~5.56% (−4 bp), 2-year ~4.77% (−2 bp).CNBC / Pomegra

Cash Treasury trading was closed Monday for Columbus Day, so Tuesday is the first session to price in last week's energy and geopolitical moves. CNBC reports the 10-year yield down more than 5 basis points at 5.1937%, the 30-year down 4 bp at 5.558% and the 2-year down more than 2 bp at 4.7658%. The trigger is energy: WTI was 1.5% lower at $92.34 by 3:50 a.m. ET, as investors priced more Russian diesel reaching world markets after the US-brokered energy ceasefire.

The 10s-30s spread is about 37 bp, so the long end is still carrying a large term premium. Last week the 10-year reached its highest level since April 2002, and it was 5.29% at the end of September, so today is a pullback from multi-decade highs, not a change in trend. Yahoo Finance's dashboard still showed 5.24% before the cash open, which shows the move came mostly from early trade. A 10-year above 5% remains the reference rate for 30-year mortgages, corporate bond spreads and equity discount rates.

Why it matters: falling oil now feeds directly into lower yields, so every headline about the diesel deal or the Gulf moves borrowing costs. If CPI comes in hot on Wednesday, this rally could reverse within hours.

September CPI on Wednesday is expected to jump to 3.7% y/y from 3.4%, with core seen at 2.5%, as the energy shock moves into consumer prices.Reuters poll via Yahoo Finance

A Reuters poll cited by Yahoo Finance expects headline CPI at 3.7% year over year, up from 3.4% in August, and core CPI (excluding food and energy) at 2.5%. The gap between the two shows that the inflation problem is mostly energy: Brent is up about 69% year over year, according to market briefs, after the Iran war shock. The Fed's preferred gauge, PCE, was 3.4% y/y in August, and its 0.3% monthly rise was the largest since May, according to Deloitte.

Consensus is a forecast, not a result, and the surprise risk now runs both ways because oil fell sharply only after the September survey period. A print above 3.7% would challenge the bond rally that started this morning. A softer core number would support the case that the Fed can stop after one more hike.

Why it matters: this is the last major inflation reading before the Oct. 27-28 FOMC meeting. It will decide whether markets price a second hike and whether 5%+ mortgage-linked rates persist into the midterms.

Sources: Yahoo Finance — Dow, Nasdaq, S&P 500 preview: Markets brace for CPI, Q3 earnings kick-off · Deloitte — Weekly Global Economic Update

The Fed is in a hiking cycle with weak hiring: September payrolls rose only 29,000, unemployment is 4.2%, and the FOMC meets Oct. 27-28.Deloitte / Schwab / UBS via Yahoo

The Fed's September increase was its first hike since 2023, and US Bank puts the policy range at 3.75%-4.00%, with markets pricing further tightening into mid-2027. Hiring has stalled at the same time. Deloitte reports September nonfarm payrolls up just 29,000, after 133,000 in August, and Schwab puts the three-month average at about 51,000, down from a peak near 142,000 in May.

Schwab has the unemployment rate at 4.2%, up slightly, while layoffs remain low, so the labor market is cooling rather than collapsing. Schwab describes new Chair Kevin Warsh as arguing that the slack in the labor market leaves room to focus on inflation. Yahoo Finance reports that traders have scaled back bets on an October hike. UBS says Fed speakers are stressing flexibility, but it still forecasts one more hike, with a risk of a second. That forecast mixes stagflation-style signals: weak jobs and 3.4%-3.7% inflation together.

Why it matters: a central bank tightening into near-zero job growth raises the odds of a policy mistake. Rate-sensitive sectors (housing, small caps, regional banks) are the most exposed if Warsh keeps his focus on inflation.

Sources: Charles Schwab — Jobs, Wages, and the Fed's Inflation Fight · U.S. Bank — Federal Reserve Monetary Policy · Yahoo Finance — Markets brace for CPI, Q3 earnings kick-off

Why it matters: the president is using sanctions policy to manage pump prices before the midterms. This creates a new driver of oil volatility that can change with a single post.

Why it matters: if Congress forces a reversal or legal challenges stall the deal, the 0.3-1 million tonnes a month of Russian diesel now priced into the market could disappear. That is a real upside risk for diesel and crude.

⚡ Energy & Commodities 4 items

Brent −$2.02 (−1.89%) to $104.63 and WTI −$1.89 (−2.02%) to $91.85, reversing Monday's ~$2 gain.Reuters via Globe and Mail / CNBC

By 6:49 a.m. ET, Brent was down $2.02 at $104.63 a barrel and WTI was down $1.89 at $91.85, Reuters reports. On Monday both benchmarks gained nearly $2, and Brent settled at its highest level since Sept. 15. The decline reflects expectations of more Russian diesel and the claimed energy-strike ceasefire, which would protect Russian refineries. Losses were limited by Houthi strikes on Riyadh's airport over the weekend and tanker attacks near the Strait of Hormuz.

Brent is still up about 69% year over year. The $13 spread between Brent and WTI shows how much of the risk premium is concentrated in seaborne, Middle East-exposed crude.

Why it matters: every $10 a barrel moves US gasoline and diesel prices and headline CPI. Today's drop helps the inflation outlook, but the Gulf risk premium keeps it fragile.

Sources: Globe and Mail (Reuters) — Oil retreats as Russian diesel supply prospects counter Gulf supply risks · CNBC — Oil prices fall as traders assess Russian diesel supply prospects and Saudi-Houthi conflict

The diesel math is small: Goldman says 800,000 t (~6m bbl) of Russian diesel would cut Gulf Coast and European wholesale prices by just $1-2 a barrel.CNBC / Ekonomichna Pravda / Reuters

Goldman Sachs estimates that an extra 800,000 metric tons of Russian diesel, about 6 million barrels, would lower wholesale diesel prices on the US Gulf Coast and in Europe by only $1 to $2 a barrel. Ekonomichna Pravda calculates that the first 300,000 tonnes (about 2.25 million barrels) would cover US transport demand of about 2.94 million barrels a day (EIA data) for less than one day.

Deputy Prime Minister Alexander Novak said on Rossiya-24 that Russian diesel exports could return to 3-3.5 million tonnes a month by year-end and claimed global diesel prices have already fallen 5-6%. PVM's Varga said that target is unlikely to be met. Russia still has a domestic export ban through the end of October, with only 427,000 t shipped to Central Asia under government agreements in September.

The diesel supply squeeze comes from Russian refinery outages, up 3 million b/d year over year according to Goldman, so the actual volumes depend on the energy ceasefire holding.

Why it matters: the diesel deal's market effect comes more from sentiment than from volume. Expect diesel crack spreads to rebound if tonnage underdelivers or Congress blocks the waiver.

Sources: Ekonomichna Pravda — Russia may export up to 3.5 mt of diesel a month by year-end · CNBC — Oil prices fall as traders assess Russian diesel supply prospects · Globe and Mail (Reuters) — Oil retreats

OPEC cuts its 2026 demand-growth forecast to 400,000 b/d from 1.4m, while Goldman raises its December Brent forecast to $90 and puts the war premium at just over $20.Reuters / CNBC

OPEC Secretary General Haitham Al Ghais said Monday that 2026 oil demand growth is now expected at 400,000 barrels a day, down from 1.4 million b/d at the start of the year. That cut of more than 70% shows high prices are destroying demand. Goldman Sachs raised its December Brent forecast to $90 from $85, while estimating that geopolitical risk adds just over $20 a barrel to current prices.

That forecast implies roughly 15% downside from today's ~$105 if risks ease. Early in 2026, Goldman had expected Brent to drift toward the low $60s on a supply glut, which shows how far the Iran war changed the market. Gold at $4,209 per ounce (Yahoo Finance) shows that inflation and geopolitical hedging demand remains elevated. All price targets here are forecasts.

Why it matters: weak demand plus a large risk premium makes the market vulnerable to a sudden drop if a Gulf or Russia de-escalation proves real, and vulnerable to spikes if it does not.

Sources: Globe and Mail (Reuters) — Oil retreats · CNBC — Oil prices fall as traders assess Russian diesel supply prospects · Hargreaves Lansdown — Goldman projects lower oil prices in 2026 as supply swells

Gulf freight shock: OPEC+ ministers say shipping and insurance costs have reached 'unprecedented levels of over one-third of the commodity value'.Reuters via Globe and Mail / BBC

OPEC and non-OPEC ministers said that shipping and insurance costs for Gulf cargoes have reached unprecedented levels of over one-third of the commodity value, Reuters reports. At Brent near $105, that means $35 or more per barrel in friction between the wellhead and the refinery. Houthi militants attacked King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh on Saturday, killing 12 and injuring more than 300, and Saudi Arabia's minister vowed a 'serious response' (BBC).

Tanker incidents near the Strait of Hormuz are raising new questions about whether Gulf exports, which had recovered to 81% of prewar levels in September, can keep recovering. Oilprice.com reports that Riyadh has pledged to keep exports flowing, which helped cool prices this morning. The freight and war-risk premium affects importers, especially Asian refiners, more than benchmark prices show.

Why it matters: high freight and insurance costs act like a tax on Asian and European energy importers, widen the Brent-WTI spread and support US export margins.
