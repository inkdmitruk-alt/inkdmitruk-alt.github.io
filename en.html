📊 Macro & Rates 4 items

Fed holds at 3.75–4.00% on a 10–2 vote; Cook and Hammack dissent for a quarter-point hike; FedWatch puts December hike odds at 87.5%CNBC / Fortune / Semafor / Yahoo Finance

The FOMC left the funds rate unchanged on October 28 after September's unanimous hike to the same range, but Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack both voted for an additional 25 bp increase — Cook's first dissent ever, which some economists read as a protest vote amid the Trump administration's legal campaign against her. The statement called inflation elevated and said 'the accumulation of more information will help the Committee determine the policy stance appropriate.' Chair Kevin Warsh was unambiguously hawkish: 'If you think that we're tolerating some inflation target somewhere above 2%, let us disabuse you of it,' adding that he does not believe in forward guidance 'absent exigent circumstances' and that politics played 'no role' six days before the midterms.

CME FedWatch moved the probability of a December 9 hike to 87.5% from 73% the day before. Two-year yields rose on the day; the 10-year touched 5.05% during the presser before slipping back under 5.00% on Thursday (−3 bp), with the 30-year at 5.30–5.33%. CNN describes a bond market caught between renewed inflation fears, an oil shock tied to Iran, widening deficits and the AI capex boom.

Why it matters: a central bank hiking into an election with the 10-year at 5% compresses equity multiples and mortgage demand simultaneously — every strong data print now raises the December-hike probability rather than the earnings outlook, which is the mechanism behind Thursday's 'good GDP, red tape' session.

Q3 GDP +3.4% annualized on the AI investment boom; September PCE 3.6% y/y (core 3.1%), spending +0.5% nominal, income only +0.2%, saving rate 3.8%CNBC / Morningstar / The Hill / NPR / MarketWatch

The advance estimate of third-quarter GDP came in at 3.4% annualized, above consensus, with The Hill attributing the upside largely to AI-related capital spending. The September PCE price index rose 0.5% m/m and 3.6% y/y (Dow Jones consensus 3.7%); core PCE rose 0.3% m/m and 3.1% y/y, up from 3.0% in August and in line with forecasts. Quarterly PCE inflation was 2.1% headline and 2.5% core.

Nominal consumer spending rose 0.5%, slightly below forecasts, while personal income rose only 0.2% and the saving rate fell to 3.8% — MarketWatch's read is that spending is 'flattening out' after the inflation surge, i.e. households are funding consumption by saving less. Initial jobless claims for the week to October 24 fell to 194,000, a level last seen in the late 1960s, but that sits against September payrolls of just +29,000 and 4.2% unemployment — low firing, low hiring. NPR's framing: five days before the midterms the data say the economy is strong, but voters say affordability is not.

Why it matters: the next data point that can change the December call is the October jobs report on November 7 — a second sub-50k payroll print would make a hike politically and economically awkward even for Warsh; a strong print locks it in.

Ukraine's central bank hikes to 16.5% and cuts its 2026 GDP forecast to −0.4% from +1.8%; inflation forecast raised to 12.5% from 10%Ekonomichna Pravda / Forbes Ukraine

The National Bank of Ukraine raised its key rate by 50 bp to 16.5% on October 29 — its second consecutive hike — and for the first time officially forecast a 2026 contraction: GDP −0.4% versus +1.8% in the July forecast. The 2026 inflation forecast was lifted to 12.5% (from 10%), easing to 7.3% in 2027. The NBU cites intensified price pressure and deteriorating expectations after Russian strikes on economic infrastructure, a collapse in exports from attacks and a de facto blockade of sea logistics, and delayed international financing tied to slipped reforms; the stated aim is to keep hryvnia assets attractive and the FX market stable.

Governor Pyshnyi said the bank could transfer up to UAH 70 billion of profit to the budget in 2027. The 2027 budget passed first reading a day earlier assumes 1.3% growth and 8% inflation — both now look optimistic. The same day Russia struck Dnipro's railway station (14 dead) and Chernihiv, and Ukraine launched 600+ drones at Moscow; Russia's own fuel shortages are easing as Ukrainian refinery strikes paused after Trump's October 11 'energy truce'.

Why it matters: Ukraine's deficit (~$37B in the draft budget) is almost entirely externally financed, so the recession call raises the stakes for the EU's €70B 2027 funding gap and the 'reparations loan' from frozen Russian assets — and the refinery-strike pause is the single biggest swing factor for global diesel margins this quarter.

ECB holds at 2.5% as Lagarde's early-exit politics resurface; Bank of England consults on tokenized collateral; Shell's $42/bbl refining margin is the inflation signal to watchCNBC / Cointelegraph / HL

CNBC reports the ECB kept its key rate at 2.5% on Thursday, with attention shifting to the politics of Christine Lagarde's possible early departure before her term ends in October 2027 and who might replace her — in July she said 'you are not going to see the back of me before 2027,' but the FT has reported otherwise. The Bank of England opened a consultation on accepting tokenized collateral for clearing, a sign that central banks are moving from pilots toward plumbing.

On the inflation side, the most telling European print was corporate: Shell nearly doubled quarterly profit year-on-year as its indicative refining margin hit a record $42 per barrel versus $24 in Q2, a direct read-through of Middle East supply disruption and Russian refinery outages into product prices. With Brent up ~11% in October to its highest since April, energy is the component keeping headline inflation sticky on both sides of the Atlantic.

Why it matters: refining margins, not crude, drive what consumers pay for diesel and jet fuel — a $42 margin means product shortages that cuts in crude prices will not fix quickly, which is why the Fed's 'elevated inflation' language has staying power.

🚢 Trade, Tariffs & Policy 3 items

Proclamation 11074: wind turbines and components declared a Section 232 national-security threat — no new tariffs yet, 180 days of negotiations, existing steel/aluminum/copper duties still applyFederal Register (Oct 22, published Oct 28)

Trump concurred with a Commerce Department finding that imports of wind turbines, components and subcomponents 'threaten to impair the national security': the report calls wind 'unreliable and intermittent,' says imported inverters are a cybersecurity vector for remote grid interference, that turbines create radar clutter affecting air defense and weather forecasting, and that U.S. reliance on imported blades and parts has grown.

Rather than impose duties now, the proclamation directs Commerce and USTR to negotiate agreements with trading partners and report within 180 days, reserving 'alternative remedies' if talks fail — the same template used before the copper and lumber actions. Section 232 tariffs on steel, aluminum and copper embedded in turbine components remain in force. The same day's Federal Register carried a Defense Production Act determination designating petroleum production, refining and logistics as 'essential to the national defense,' waiving standard DPA procedures and authorizing the Secretary of War to make purchases and financial commitments to expand capacity — the industrial-policy mirror image of the wind action.

Why it matters: the 180-day clock runs to April 2027, leaving U.S. offshore and onshore wind developers under tariff threat through the next construction season; the DPA move signals federal financing for refining capacity, which bears directly on the distillate squeeze.

Executive Order 14438 on housing costs: automatic PMI cancellation pilot at Fannie/Freddie, a manufactured-housing task force with 180-day deadline, automated title review replacing title insurance on refinancesFederal Register (EO 14438)

The order directs the FHFA director — subject to a safety-and-soundness finding — to have Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac run a pilot that uses automated valuation models to identify borrowers who have reached the equity threshold and cancel private mortgage insurance proactively, with servicers required to notify borrowers and drop documentation and appraisal hurdles. It creates a Task Force on Manufactured Housing Innovation (HUD and FHFA co-chairs, with USDA, Commerce and VA) to recommend within 180 days reciprocal recognition of certifications across federal programs, elimination of duplicative inspections for factory-built housing, and whether federal grants can be conditioned on standardized state permitting.

FHFA is to convert the 'title acceptance' pilot into a permanent nationwide program accepting automated title-risk assessment in lieu of lender's title insurance on eligible refinances, extend it above 80% LTV, waive appraisals and income re-verification on rate-reducing refinances, and cut loan-level price adjustments — with savings passed to borrowers. USDA and VA are told to lower rates on their direct loans. The preamble invokes 'Super Intelligence' risk models.

Why it matters: this is a demand-side cost cut aimed at title insurers and PMI providers rather than at rates — with the 10-year at 5% and mortgages well above 6.5%, it is the administration's substitute for the rate relief the Fed is not delivering before the election.

DHS suspends visa-free entry for Chinese nationals to the Northern Mariana Islands from October 30; UFLPA list adds three Chinese genomics firms; ITC schedules steel-plate sunset reviews on China, Russia, UkraineDHS / Newsweek / SCMP / Federal Register

DHS's October 27 notice suspends the CNMI Economic Vitality & Security Travel Authorization Program — 14-day visa-free entry for pre-screened PRC nationals — effective 2 p.m. EDT October 30, including for travelers with existing approvals; over 3,000 PRC nationals used it in FY2025 and Chinese visitors were more than 20% of FY2026 arrivals, so the islands' tourism authority is 'deeply concerned' while Senator Mike Lee hails the closure of a 'birth tourism' loophole.

On October 28 DHS, for the Forced Labor Enforcement Task Force, added three Chinese genomics companies to the UFLPA Entity List, creating a rebuttable presumption that their goods are made with forced labor. The ITC's October 29 Federal Register notice schedules expedited five-year reviews of anti-dumping orders on cut-to-length carbon steel plate from China, Russia and Ukraine.

Separately, NPR reports automakers are flagging tariffs, diesel and chips as the three cost pressures in this earnings season.

Why it matters: each of these is small on its own, but together they show trade policy running on autopilot in the final week before the midterms — enforcement actions continue regardless of the Vance-letter diplomacy or the earnings tape.
