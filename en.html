📊 Macro & Rates 4 items

Long-end Treasury yields hit 24-year highs as a global bond selloff acceleratesNBC News / Bloomberg / CNBC

On Wednesday, October 7, the 30-year Treasury yield reached 5.73%, the highest since 2002, and the 10-year peaked at 5.36%. A $39 billion 10-year auction cleared at 5.3%, the highest yield on a 10-year sale since November 2000, though strong demand pulled yields off their highs. The selloff was global: the UK 30-year gilt hit its highest level since 1998, and French and Italian yields jumped.

Ed Yardeni warned that France may be nearing a debt crisis. The drivers were oil above $100, sticky inflation, large fiscal deficits and heavy AI-related corporate borrowing. By Friday the 30-year had eased to about 5.60% and the 10-year to about 5.24%.

Why it matters: a 10-year above 5% raises mortgage, corporate and government borrowing costs and compresses equity valuations, which makes rates the main risk to the bull market.

Sources: NBC News — Treasury yields hit 24-year highs · Bloomberg — US 30-year yields return to 2002 high · CNBC — Treasury yields steady as traders wrap up volatile week

Labor market stalls: September payrolls +29,000, unemployment at 4.2%BabyPips / Bloomberg

Nonfarm payrolls rose just 29,000 in September, below every estimate in a forecast range of roughly 84,000 to 95,000. Unemployment rose to 4.2%, partly because the labor force grew. Revisions were negative: August was cut to +133,000 from +162,000, and July now shows a loss of 10,000 jobs. Average hourly earnings rose only 0.1% on the month to $37.81, a 3.0% annual pace. With PCE inflation at 3.4%, real wage growth is now close to zero or negative.

The University of Michigan’s preliminary October sentiment index fell to 46.3, against a 48 consensus and the lowest since May.

Why it matters: weak hiring with 3%+ inflation is stagflation-like, and it pulls the Fed between its two mandates.

Sources: BabyPips — US jobs report September 2026 · Yahoo Finance — Market wrap Oct 9 (UMich sentiment)

Trump’s Russia–Ukraine “energy ceasefire” is unconfirmed but matters for dieselNBC News / Axios / France 24

On Sunday, October 11, Trump posted on Truth Social that an “ENERGY CEASEFIRE” between Russia and Ukraine was in effect and that both sides had agreed. He gave no details. Zelenskyy told Axios the deal was news to him but said Ukraine would comply if Russia did. The Kremlin has not confirmed it, and a similar announcement on September 14 was followed by continued strikes. The economic channel is refining: the IEA identified Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries, together with restricted Hormuz flows, as key drivers of the global diesel squeeze.

A real pause could ease product cracks and diesel prices, though Russian strikes on Ukraine’s grid would also have to stop.

Why it matters: if it holds, the truce is a bigger potential downward force on diesel prices than the import deal, so watch for Moscow’s confirmation and refinery-strike data.

🚢 Trade, Tariffs & Policy 3 items

OFAC’s General License 135 opens a path for Russian diesel imports through April 7, 2027Ship & Bunker / NPR

After a Trump–Putin call, Treasury’s Office of Foreign Assets Control issued General License 135. It authorizes transactions related to the sale, delivery and importation, including into the United States, of Russian-origin diesel through April 7, 2027. It is the first such license since the war began to run longer than the usual 30 days. Trump said Russia would supply over 300,000 tonnes immediately, 500,000 tonnes in November and 1 million tonnes after that.

The White House did not say who pays or when the fuel arrives. EU and UK import bans and the $100/bbl product price cap remain in force, so the license mainly opens US and third-country channels.

Why it matters: this is a supply-side inflation tool used for political ends, and it weakens the West’s sanctions leverage over Moscow at the same time.

Sources: Ship & Bunker — US lifts sanctions on Russian diesel for six months · NPR — Trump says U.S. to get diesel from Russia
