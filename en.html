Why it matters: the hold is priced; the 2-year's reaction to Warsh's December language at 2:30 p.m. Wednesday is the single most important print of the week for every asset class.

Asked Sunday about a strike on Iran, Trump said 'there will not be a strike today' and described the choice as between making a deal and the option to 'finish them off', according to Reuters-sourced reports. The mechanism runs through inflation expectations: a $6–7 move in Brent is worth roughly 0.1–0.2 points of headline CPI over the following months, which is exactly the margin the Fed is arguing about for December.

Nothing has been announced on Truth Social that media have quoted on markets this weekend; the Iran line is the market-moving statement of record.

Why it matters: 'not today' is the cheapest possible put option for equities — it lifts futures without committing to anything — and the market will demand a firmer statement or a Qatari outcome before taking the oil premium out for good.

Why it matters: a pre-midterm auto deal would lift Detroit supply-chain names and the loonie and remove a cost-of-living input the administration is being punished for; the gap between Hassett's 'very close' and the official's 'insufficient' is the trade to watch this week.
