📊 Macro & Rates 4 items

September CPI beats on core: 0.3% m/m and 2.6% y/yCoinDesk / The Hill / Yahoo

Headline CPI rose 0.6% m/m, matching forecasts, and 3.7% y/y versus 3.6% expected and 3.4% in August. Core CPI rose 0.3% m/m versus 0.2% expected, and the annual rate climbed to 2.6% from 2.4%. Gasoline rose 9.1% in the month and accounted for roughly half of the headline increase, an energy shock tied to the Iran war. Analysts cited shelter, airfares and used cars as the drivers of the core surprise, which suggests pass-through beyond fuel.

Inflation has outpaced wage growth for the past five months, per AP-cited reporting, so real incomes are being squeezed. Higher real and nominal rates hurt rate-sensitive sectors and long-duration equities, while indexed income streams such as Social Security catch up with a lag.

Why it matters: the core beat removes the Fed's ability to call this a transitory energy shock and makes the next PCE print and the Beige Book (due this afternoon) pivotal for the Oct 27–28 FOMC.

Sources: CoinDesk CPI recap · The Hill

Bloomberg reported the 2-year yield up 12bp at 4.90%, the biggest mover on the curve. CoinDesk's early read had the 10-year up 9.3bp to 5.308%, and Yahoo Finance put the intraday high at 5.33% before it eased to about 5.23–5.25%. The 30-year moved less: sources range from about 5.54% to 5.61%, against Tuesday's 5.558%, which flattens the 2s/30s curve. A flattening led by the front end means markets are pricing Fed action more than a fiscal term premium.

The dollar index rose about 0.7% to 102.85 and gold fell about 1.8% to around $4,103. Mortgage rates followed: the MBA 30-year contract rate was 7.5%, and its mortgage market index fell to 199.2 from 204.7.

Why it matters: a 10-year holding above about 5.3% tightens financial conditions for housing, autos and leveraged AI borrowers, and could do part of the Fed's job for it.

Sources: US Treasuries Slump as Hot Inflation Lifts Fed Rate-Hike Bets · 10-year Treasury yield jumps on hot core (CNBC)

Housing squeeze: Fannie Mae lifts its 2027 mortgage-rate forecast to 7.5%, and the 2027 COLA is 3.6%CNBC / SSA

CNBC reported that Fannie Mae now expects the 30-year fixed rate to average 7.4% in Q4 2026 and 7.5% in 2027, up from 6.8% and 6.7% a month ago. We found no second outlet confirming those figures. Mortgage News Daily had the average 30-year at 7.46% on Tuesday, before the CPI-driven rise in yields. Separately, the SSA announced a 3.6% COLA for 2027, the largest since 2023's 8.7%.

It lifts the average retired-worker benefit from $2,095 to $2,170 a month for about 76 million Social Security and SSI recipients. The taxable wage cap rises to $192,300 from $184,500, a stealth tax increase on higher earners. The COLA is tied to Q3 CPI-W, so it locks in this year's energy-driven inflation for 2027 federal outlays.

Why it matters: higher mortgage rates and indexed transfer spending together put upward pressure on deficits and term premia while housing turnover stays frozen.

🌐 Geopolitics & the Economy 3 items

Second merchant ship lost near Hormuz since the war beganReuters / Arab News

The Singapore-flagged container ship GFS Pride (2,824 TEU) sank near the Strait of Hormuz, according to maritime security sources and a Pakistani navigational warning dated Oct 14. All 21 crew were evacuated. Reuters sources say a projectile hit the ship, while Iran's IRGC claims it struck a naval mine, which risk firm Marisks called unverified. IMO data make it the second vessel sunk since the US-Iran war began on Feb 28, and the bulk carrier Cape Dao is reported to be sinking after a Sept 23 hit.

UKMTO reports more than 20 vessels, mostly tankers, attacked this month in Hormuz and the Gulf of Oman. Two senior Iranian officials told Reuters that the new tactics aim to keep oil prices high and influence the US midterms.

Why it matters: Iran is deliberately using the shipping channel to put an inflation tax on the US electorate, so war-risk premia and freight costs are likely to stay high at least until Nov 3.

Sources: Container ship sinks near Hormuz (Reuters via Arab News)

Saudi Arabia's Energy Ministry said an Aramco administrative building in Dhahran was targeted on Tuesday evening, causing a limited fire with no casualties. A company source said the top three floors of an office tower were damaged. That threatens the Red Sea bypass routes that eased the Hormuz bottleneck, including East-West pipeline flows to Yanbu.

Why it matters: if the Red Sea outlet is also contested, the Gulf loses its main alternative export route, which lifts the floor under Brent.

Sources: Houthis vow more attacks after Aramco HQ hit (Reuters via Investing.com) · Houthi attack hits Aramco's historic nerve centre (The National)

Why it matters: crude volumes are recovering, but any US-Iran naval clash would erase that recovery in one session, which helps explain why option skew in oil stays bid.
