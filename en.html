🧭 The throughline

The market is repricing two regime changes at once. Politically, a Democratic sweep of Congress turns Washington into a divided government that will contest tariffs, Iran war funding and the December 11 budget deadline, and oil has already dumped its war premium on the view that the conflict will be constrained. Economically, a 165,000 October payrolls print with unemployment at 4.1% and inflation at 3.7% has flipped the Fed debate from cuts to a likely December hike, pushing the 10-year to 4.85%. Caught between higher discount rates and doubts about AI capex payback, the Nasdaq slipped into correction before a Friday chip bounce. The setup for the coming weeks is asymmetric: oil has priced peace, bonds have priced a hike, and AI megacaps have priced perfection, so the surprises that matter most are an Iran flare-up, a hot CPI, or another wobble in AI spending.

📊 Macro & Rates 4 items

October payrolls +165K crush consensus; unemployment falls to 4.1%BLS / Yahoo Finance / Morningstar

The BLS reported 165,000 jobs added in October against consensus of roughly 59,000–70,000, with August and September revised up by a combined 84,000. Unemployment slipped to 4.1% from 4.2%, staying in the 4.1–4.3% band it has held since March. Average hourly earnings rose 0.4% on the month and 3.1% year over year. With inflation running at 3.7%, real wages are still falling, which is a squeeze on households but not a signal of labor-market slack.

The report removes the main argument doves had for waiting: there is no visible cooling in hiring. It also undercuts the narrative that tariffs and the Iran war oil shock were tipping the economy toward recession.

Why it matters: a strong labor market alongside 3.7% inflation means the Fed's next move is more likely up than down, which raises borrowing costs across mortgages, credit cards and corporate debt and pressures long-duration growth stocks.

Sources: Yahoo Finance · Morningstar · StockTitan

Why it matters: a December hike would extend the high-rate regime into 2027, keep the dollar firm, and raise the hurdle rate for every capex-heavy story, AI infrastructure included.

Shutdown clock: government funded only through December 11CREFC / Multibagg

The current continuing resolution funds the federal government through December 11, 2026. As of November 3, the Senate had passed none of the 12 FY2027 appropriations bills. Lawmakers return November 9 for a lame-duck session, while the new Democratic-majority Congress is sworn in January 3. Democrats now have an incentive to delay a full-year deal until they control both chambers, which raises the odds of another stopgap or a lapse.

The US has had three funding lapses since October 2025, including a 43-day shutdown, so markets know the playbook: delayed data releases, slower government payments, and a short-term bid for T-bills.

Why it matters: a December shutdown could delay the very data (jobs, CPI) the Fed needs for its December 9 decision and adds fiscal risk premium at the long end.

Sources: CREFC · Multibagg
