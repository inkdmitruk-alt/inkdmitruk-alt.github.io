📊 Macro & Rates 5 items

Stocks bounce on a tech rally while Treasury yields stay above 5%.Reuters / CoinDesk / Benzinga

The S&P 500 closed at 7,690.73 (+0.41%), the Nasdaq Composite at 26,430.89 (+1.11%) and the Dow at 51,966.03 (-0.07%). The gains were narrow: a tech and AI rebound did the lifting while blue chips lagged. Sources disagree on yields. Reuters credited the rebound partly to lower government bond yields and easier crude. CoinDesk’s live blog had the 10-year up 3.3 bp to 5.133%, and Benzinga reported it about 6 bp higher at 5.14%.

The 10-year had fallen about 14.6 bp the previous week and briefly approached 5%. The VIX fell about 5% to just under 19 after jumping about 20% on Friday. Europe did worse, with the Stoxx 600 down 0.8% and the CAC 40 down 1.65%. With the 10-year still near its highest levels since 2007, equity valuations are being set by the discount rate rather than earnings momentum.

Why it matters: A rally in long-duration tech while yields are flat to higher depends on positioning, not on easier financial conditions. Watch whether the 10-year stays below the 5.3% it touched after the CPI release. A break above that level would likely undo Monday’s rebound in the most heavily borrowed AI names first.

Before the October 27–28 FOMC meeting, markets price only about a one-in-six chance of a hike, but the inflation backdrop remains hot.CNBC / Yahoo Finance / CoinDesk / Invezz

September CPI, released October 14, rose 3.7% from a year earlier, up from 3.4% in August and above the 3.6% consensus. Prices rose 0.6% on the month, and core prices rose 0.3% against a 0.2% forecast. PPI followed with a 1.3% monthly jump, against roughly 0.5–0.6% expected. The Fed raised rates in September for the first time in more than three years, to a 3.75%–4% target range.

After CPI, odds of an October hike reached about 42–54%, depending on the outlet. Since then, dovish signals from Chair Kevin Warsh have pulled them down. CoinDesk put Monday’s odds at 18%, up from a 12% low last week, and Invezz cited 17.2%. Energy is driving the inflation: diesel prices have risen almost 70% since the Iran war began in February, according to NBC.

Why it matters: The Fed is likely to skip October and keep December open, which leaves front-end yields vulnerable to every oil headline. That is why Washington’s Russia sanctions waiver counts as monetary news too. Watch October flash PMIs and any Warsh comments before the blackout period.

Junk-bond stress is rising, with AI-infrastructure debt at the center.MarketWatch / Dow Jones / Bloomberg Law / Reuters

MarketWatch reports that credit markets have entered ‘uncharted territory’ as the riskiest junk bonds flash a warning. Bloomberg Law reported in late September that spreads on US CCC-rated bonds were approaching 1,000 basis points. Market watchers expect more widening if further Fed hikes squeeze earnings and refinancing. AI borrowing is now the clearest pressure point. CoreWeave’s $2.75 billion of 9.75% notes issued in April traded at 81 cents on the dollar, yielding more than 15%, during last week’s selloff, according to Dow Jones.

S&P rates CoreWeave B+. On Monday its 2031 bond yield fell 19.5 bp to 15.13% (Tradeweb data cited by Reuters). Reuters puts CoreWeave’s debt at about $50 billion, and The Block cites $35.6 billion outstanding at the end of June. Oracle, the only non-investment-grade name among the large AI-capex issuers, had 2032 notes yielding about 7.24% after its shares fell nearly 9% in the selloff.

Why it matters: The test is whether stress spreads from B-rated neoclouds to investment-grade hyperscaler spreads. If it does, the AI capex cycle starts to cost more to fund, and data-center buildouts slow. Watch record CDS levels on CoreWeave and Oracle and new-issue concessions on AI-linked debt.

Homebuilder sentiment drops to 29 as 5%-plus long rates hit housing.NAHB via CoinDesk

The NAHB Housing Market Index fell to 29 in October from 32. Forecasts had expected no change. Some 43% of builders cut prices this month, the highest share recorded in the 2020–2026 period. The transmission is direct: with the 10-year near 5.1%, 30-year fixed mortgage rates reached about 7.45% in late September, their highest since April 2024, according to a CNBC-cited report.

Price cuts protect sales volume at the expense of margins. That points to lower residential investment and weaker earnings for builders and building-products companies in Q4.

Why it matters: Housing is the sector most sensitive to Fed and term-premium moves. Weak starts and permits this week would support the view that the September hike is already biting, which would argue against a second move. Watch builder guidance in this earnings season.

Japan’s PM Takaichi rejects the ‘Takaichi weak yen’ label as JGB yields sit at multi-decade highs.Bloomberg / CNBC / Seoul Economic Daily / Kyodo

In the upper house on October 19, Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi said she ‘categorically’ rejected criticism that her policies cause a weak yen and high interest rates. She said a strong economy would restore confidence in the currency. She pledged to ‘appropriately control’ annual bond issuance but did not rule out issuing government bonds to fund a cut in the food consumption tax.

Kyodo polling shows cabinet approval down to 49.1%, the lowest since she took office.

Why it matters: Debt-funded tax cuts with yields this high could keep pressure on the long end of the JGB curve. That would pull Japanese money home and add to upward pressure on US term premia. Watch 30-year JGB auctions and USD/JPY near intervention levels.

The Future Investment Initiative, known as ‘Davos in the Desert’ and scheduled for October 26–29 in Riyadh, has been moved to 2027. Speakers had included JPMorgan’s Jamie Dimon, Citi’s Jane Fraser, Goldman’s David Solomon and BlackRock’s Larry Fink. The delay hurts the kingdom’s effort to attract foreign capital and diversify away from oil.

Why it matters: Attacks on Saudi energy sites keep a security premium in Brent, which is still around $105–110. Watch whether Gulf sovereign funds slow foreign deployments or pivot to domestic spending, and whether insurers reprice Red Sea and Gulf cover again.

Russian strikes have cost Ukrainian businesses $7.7 billion in 2026, and power rationing is spreading to industry.Kyiv Independent / Suspilne / Ekonomichna Pravda

Prime Minister Serhii Koretskyi put 2026 business losses from Russian attacks at $7.7 billion. The total covers premises, factories and warehoused goods, and more than 1 million square meters of mostly logistics real estate has been destroyed or badly damaged. Ukrenergo scheduled hourly household outages on October 20 from 06:00 to 23:59 and capacity limits for industry in all regions. Poland’s Cersanit fully halted its Zviahel plant after a repeat strike.

Why it matters: A VAT increase plus rationed power is a stagflationary mix for Ukraine’s economy going into winter, raising the need for external budget support. Watch the IMF program reviews, EU financing decisions, and whether foreign manufacturers follow Cersanit in suspending Ukrainian operations.

🚢 Trade, Tariffs & Policy 4 items

China’s Q3 GDP grows 4.5% on an export surge while domestic demand stays weak.NBS via Xinhua / FXStreet / Vantage / Eastern Herald

China’s economy grew 4.5% year on year in Q3, beating the 4.4% consensus and accelerating from 4.3% in Q2, the weakest pace in about three years. Quarter-on-quarter growth was 1.2%, up from 0.9%. Nine-month growth of 4.6% sits within the 4.5%–5% target. Exports drove the beat: September shipments reached $447.18 billion, up 36.3% from a year earlier, and the January–September trade surplus was about $938 billion.

Domestic demand remained weak. Retail sales rose only 1.1% in September, and fixed-asset investment fell 7.2% to 33.41 trillion yuan, though industrial output rose 5.5%. Analysts quoted by Seoul Economic Daily said the beat reduces pressure for aggressive Q4 stimulus.

Why it matters: A $938 billion surplus built on export volume adds to trade friction and pushes deflation abroad. It may also come up at the Trump–Xi–Putin meeting Trump floated. Less stimulus also means less support for metals demand. Watch the yuan fixing and any Western trade defenses on Chinese goods.

Indonesia’s stock market is being repriced over a possible MSCI downgrade.Trading Economics / CNBC / China Daily (Bloomberg)

The IDX Composite fell 270 points (5.2%) to 4,915 early Monday, its third straight decline and weakest level since October 2020. It had lost about 10.8% on Friday, the steepest one-day fall since January 1998, which triggered a 30-minute trading halt. The trigger is MSCI’s consultation on moving Indonesia from Emerging to Standalone market status, citing transparency, concentrated ownership and insider trading.

A decision is due by June 30, 2027. Goldman Sachs estimates passive outflows of up to $13 billion. Regulators cut the daily auto-rejection limit to 7% from 15%, plan to raise the minimum free float to 15%, and fined coordinated-trading cases IDR 2.1 trillion. Bank Central Asia fell 6.9% on Monday.

Why it matters: A forced passive exit of that size could weaken the rupiah and push Bank Indonesia to defend the currency with rates. Other frontier-adjacent markets will be watched for similar governance reviews. The thing to watch is how credible the reforms look before MSCI decides.

Black Sea trade route: Kyiv will cover up to 80% of ship damage, and Russia rejects a neutral-flag food corridor.Kyiv Independent / Reuters

Ukraine’s Infrastructure Ministry will compensate shipowners, operators and charterers, whether Ukrainian or foreign-flagged, for up to 80% of assessed Russian strike damage that insurers do not cover. Covered losses include hull, cargo and rescue costs. Turkey, India and Egypt offered to carry food from Ukrainian and Russian ports under their own flags, but Zelensky told Bloomberg that Moscow declined to guarantee safe passage.

Russia hit 52 vessels in six weeks in July–August. Romania says four merchant ships were struck in 24 hours on October 15, killing eight people. London’s marine market widened its high-risk zone on September 18, and Black Sea premiums have risen sharply. According to the agriculture ministry, about 32 million tonnes of farm products worth roughly $10 billion are at risk because the Danube and rail routes lack capacity.

Why it matters: Without enough Black Sea capacity, Ukraine loses export earnings and wheat and corn supply tightens just as fuel costs lift farm input prices. Watch whether state compensation brings shippers back, and how grain futures respond to any corridor talks.

DHS orders DeepSeek removed from federal IT systems, widening the US–China tech wall.The Hill / Ekonomichna Pravda

The Department of Homeland Security ordered the removal of Chinese AI model DeepSeek from government agency IT systems, according to The Hill. 🎙 Trump & Policy Signals 3 items

Trump defends waiving Graham Act sanctions on Russia, and oil falls about 2%.NBC / CNBC / The Hill / IBTimes (Yahoo) / Ukrainska Pravda

Trump’s October 16 executive order waived the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026 two days before it took effect. The law would have allowed tariffs of up to 100% on all goods from the five largest buyers of Russian oil and gas, potentially including China and India. It passed the Senate 86–11, a margin Sen. Tillis highlighted. It is the second Russia waiver in two weeks and follows a Trump–Putin deal to release hundreds of thousands of tons of Russian diesel onto world markets.

On Monday, Ukrainska Pravda quoted Trump saying that lifting the sanctions helps end the war. He called the waiver ‘a very good decision’ on Saturday, according to NBC. The market reaction was immediate: Brent fell almost 2% to $107.69 and WTI 1.71% to $97.32 by 9:27 a.m. ET (IBTimes via Yahoo), and Reuters cited easing crude as one support for the tech rebound. Rubio said Trump wants 60 days to study the sanctions’ economic impact.

Why it matters: Removing the threat of secondary tariffs on China and India takes a major trade-war risk off the table and weakens the pressure on Russian oil revenue. Watch Urals discounts, Indian and Chinese crude purchases, and whether Congress tries to override the waiver after the November 3 midterms.

That would lower geopolitical risk premia for China-exposed assets and oil importers. Watch APEC logistics and any tariff concessions announced beforehand.

In a brief filed Friday, the CFTC argued that CME lacks standing to challenge its policy allowing crypto perpetual futures. It said CME claims competitive harm without showing imminent financial injury, and asked a federal judge to dismiss the case, as it requested in September. CME argues that perps have no expiry, so they are swaps rather than futures and fall under a different regime.

The CFTC approved the first US bitcoin perp, Kalshi’s, in May 2026 and regulates them case by case as futures. It also says volume in CME’s own crypto futures has risen since the policy change.
